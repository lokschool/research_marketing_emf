## 2. Pain Point Analysis

### Pain Point 1: Lean Team Running Growing Conference
**Evidence:** 16 employees organizing Hong Kong's leading sustainability conference. Single flagship event annually. All revenue concentrated in one event.
**Impact:** Team stretched thin during event season. No bandwidth for year-round programming. Single point of failure for event execution. Cannot scale to additional events without hiring.
**Urgency:** High — growth constrained by team size.

### Pain Point 2: No Year-Round Sustainability Community Platform
**Evidence:** ReThink HK happens once annually. ESG/sustainability is a year-round business imperative — but the community only engages during conference days.
**Impact:** Massive missed opportunity for year-round content, networking, and community monetization. Attendees and sponsors get episodic value. Competitors (or media companies) could capture the HK sustainability community between events.
**Urgency:** High — community ownership at risk.

### Pain Point 3: Manual Delegate & Sponsor Acquisition
**Evidence:** 16-person team likely doing manual outreach to corporate ESG leaders, government officials, NGOs, and green tech vendors.
**Impact:** Cannot scale attendee and sponsor acquisition without proportional headcount increase. Each new corporate sponsor requires manual sales cycles. No automated lead qualification.
**Urgency:** Medium — growth bottleneck.

### Pain Point 4: No Post-Event Engagement or Lead Nurturing
**Evidence:** No evidence of automated follow-up, lead nurturing, or post-event attendee engagement.
**Impact:** Warm leads from conference go cold. Sponsors and exhibitors get no post-event value. Attendees receive no personalized follow-up content. Event ROI for all stakeholders is limited to 2-3 conference days.
**Urgency:** Medium — stakeholder retention risk.

### Pain Point 5: Competition from Larger Events Adding Sustainability Tracks
**Evidence:** BEYOND Expo, HKTDC events, and major trade shows increasingly add ESG/sustainability content. ReThink HK's niche is being encroached upon by larger, better-resourced organizers.
**Impact:** Risk of losing market position as sustainability becomes mainstream. Larger organizers can cross-subsidize sustainability content with other revenue streams.
**Urgency:** Medium — competitive threat.
